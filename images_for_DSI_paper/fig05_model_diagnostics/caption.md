**Figure 5.** Model-based diagnostics by estimation window, and their correspondence with the DSIr screen. (A) Retrospective trajectories of estimated biomass under the full-history and post-1987 windows for neritic tuna and sardines: black, the fit to all data; light blue, refits with one to five terminal years removed; dark points, the terminal estimate of each peel. (B) Mohn's ρ on terminal-year biomass by functional group and window (colour clipped at ±1; values printed), with three summary columns: mean |ρ| over the eight groups, the same excluding neritic tuna, and mean |ρ| computed on MSY instead of biomass. The full history and the post-1987 window carry the largest biomass revisions, each driven by one group (neritic tuna and sardines respectively); the DSIr group-optimum, Thai DOF and full-history windows have the smallest MSY revisions. (C) The model refitted with every global start-year floor from 1971 to 2010 (window ending in 2023; five peels per floor). Top: mean |Mohn's ρ| on biomass (all groups, and excluding neritic tuna) and on MSY; red crosses mark floors at which at least one fit lacked a positive-definite Hessian. Middle: hindcast error of the survey index from the peel fits under observed effort, as mean absolute scaled error on the log scale (one year ahead across five origins; five years ahead from 2018), where values below 1 beat a no-change forecast. Bottom: DSIr of the three aggregated commercial groups from Fig. 3 for the same start years. Dashed verticals mark the candidate starts 1977, 1988 and 2001. Biomass-based retrospective stability is best for floors between 1978 and 1987 and becomes erratic for later floors as single groups jump between solutions; MSY-based stability deteriorates steadily for floors after about 1990; hindcast skill is best for floors in the mid to late 1990s and declines for floors after 2003. The DSIr maximum of the pelagic group (starts 2008–2011) coincides with the least reliable region of the model, whereas the demersal DSIr maximum (1977) lies inside the most stable region: the screen identifies contrast, not reliability.
