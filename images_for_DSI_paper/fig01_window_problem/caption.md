**Figure 1.** Why the estimation window matters and what the Data Suitability Index measures, illustrated with the aggregated demersal group of the Gulf of Thailand commercial fishery. (A) Three candidate estimation windows, each ending in the last observed year (top), with commercial catch per unit effort (CPUE, middle) and fishing effort (bottom), 1971–2024. The dashed line marks the 1987 downward break detected in the fishery-independent research-vessel (RV) index (Fig. 2). Each window bar has the colour of the years it adds: grey, years up to 1987 (used only by the full history); light blue, 1988–2015; dark blue, 2016–2024 (used by every window). Points in A and B use the same colours. (B) Effort against log(CPUE) within each window; the line is the ordinary least-squares fit and β its slope (per 10⁶ effort units). The full-history slope (β = −0.040) is dominated by the difference between the pre-1987 and post-1987 clusters rather than by a within-regime response to effort; the post-1987 window has less contrast but a single cloud (β = −0.017); the recent window has only nine points (β = −0.047). (C) DSI (open circles) and its robustness-adjusted version DSIr (filled circles) for the same three windows in each aggregated commercial group, each group scored against its own effort series; the bar between them is the robustness discount. The anchovy series starts in 1972, so its full-history window is 1972–2024. DSI rewards contrast and therefore scores the full history highly in the demersal group (DSI 91), whereas DSIr discounts fragile windows (DSIr 45; 13 for the recent window). Neither index measures whether a window represents the current system; that is assessed with the model-based diagnostics in Figs 5 and 6. The weighted components of DSI are shown in Fig. S1.
