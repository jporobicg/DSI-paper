**Figure 1.** Why the estimation window matters and what the Data Suitability Index measures, illustrated with the aggregated demersal group of the Gulf of Thailand commercial fishery. (A) Commercial catch per unit effort (CPUE, upper) and fishing effort (lower), 1971–2024. The dashed line marks the 1987 downward break detected in the fishery-independent research-vessel index (Fig. 2). Brackets show three candidate estimation windows, each ending in the last year: the full history (1971–2024), the post-break window (1988–2024) and the recent window (2016–2024). (B) The effort–log(CPUE) relationship within each window; points are coloured by period and the line is the ordinary least-squares fit with slope β (per 10⁶ effort units). The full-history slope is dominated by the difference between the pre-1987 and post-1987 clusters rather than by a within-regime response to effort; the post-break window has less contrast but a single cloud; the recent window has few points. (C) Decomposition of DSI and DSIr for the three windows in each aggregated group. The upper bar stacks the weighted base components (depletion slope 0.35, effort contrast 0.20, CPUE contrast 0.20, sample size 0.10, catch–effort coherence 0.10); the black tick is DSI after the outlier penalty; the lower bar is DSIr, obtained by multiplying DSI by the robustness factors for missing-data coverage, regression influence, leave-one-out slope stability and fit–effort coherence. DSI rewards contrast and therefore scores the full history highly in the demersal group; DSIr discounts fragile windows. Neither index measures whether a window represents the current system; that is assessed with the model-based diagnostics in Fig. 5.
