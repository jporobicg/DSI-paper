**Figure 5.** Retrospective stability, in-sample fit and hindcast skill by estimation window. (A) Retrospective trajectories of estimated biomass for neritic tuna and sardines under the full-history and post-1987 windows: black, the fit to all data; light blue, refits with one to five terminal years removed ("peels"); dark points, the terminal estimate of each peel. Trajectories start in each window's first modelled year. (B) Mohn's ρ on terminal-year biomass over the five peels (three for the recent window, which cannot be peeled further), by functional group (columns) and window (rows). Orange cells are upward revisions as data are removed and blue cells downward ones; colour saturates at ±1 and the printed values are unclipped. The symbol beside each window matches Fig. 4. (C) Summaries by window, aligned with the rows of B: mean |Mohn's ρ| over the eight groups on biomass and on MSY; the in-sample log root-mean-square error of the fitted research-vessel CPUE over the common window 2016–2023 (years inside every fitted window, so this measures fit, not prediction); and the hindcast mean absolute scaled error (MASE) of the survey index one year ahead, pooled over the five peel origins, where values below 1 beat a no-change forecast (21 predictions from three origins for the recent window, 35 from five origins for the others). The full history (mean |ρ| 0.42) and the post-1987 window (0.48) carry the largest biomass revisions, each driven by one group (neritic tuna, ρ = 2.23, and sardines, ρ = 2.68, respectively); without neritic tuna the full-history value falls to 0.16. The DSIr group-optimum (0.13) and Thai DOF (0.19) windows are the most stable on biomass, and the DSIr group-optimum, full-history and Thai DOF windows have the smallest MSY revisions. In-sample fit and hindcast skill differ little between windows except for the recent window, which fits its own short period best. All results use the corrected catch scaling.
