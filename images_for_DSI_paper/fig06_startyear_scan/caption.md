**Figure 6.** The model refitted with every global start-year floor from 1971 to 2010 (window ending in 2023; five retrospective peels per floor), compared with the DSIr screen. (A) Mean |Mohn's ρ| over the eight groups on terminal biomass (black), on terminal biomass excluding neritic tuna (grey) and on MSY (dark blue). Crosses above the panel mark floors at which at least one of the six fits (full data or a peel) lacked a positive-definite Hessian. (B) Hindcast error of the survey index from the peel fits under observed effort, as mean absolute scaled error on the log scale: one year ahead pooled over five origins, and all horizons up to five years ahead from the 2018 origin. Values below 1 (horizontal line) beat a no-change forecast. (C) DSIr of the three aggregated commercial groups (Fig. 3) for the same start years. Dashed verticals mark the candidate starts 1977, 1988 and 2001. Biomass-based retrospective stability is best for floors between 1978 and 1987 (mean |ρ| about 0.1), is high for floors up to 1977 because of neritic tuna, and becomes erratic for later floors as single groups jump between solutions; the floor of 1988 itself (the post-1987 window) is a local spike (0.48) between two stable floors (1987: 0.09; 1989: 0.12). MSY-based stability deteriorates for floors after about 1990, and hindcast skill five years ahead is best for floors in the mid to late 1990s and declines for floors after 2003. The DSIr maximum of the pelagic group (starts 2008–2011) coincides with the least reliable region of the model, whereas the demersal DSIr maximum (1977) lies at the edge of the most stable region: the screen identifies contrast, not reliability.
