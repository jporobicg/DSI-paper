**Figure 3.** Data Suitability Index screening of candidate estimation windows. (A–C) DSI (thin light line) and DSIr (thick dark line) for every window that starts in the year shown and ends in the last observed year, for the anchovy, demersal and pelagic groups of the Thai Department of Fisheries (DOF) commercial statistics; shading between the lines is the robustness discount. Each group is scored against its own effort series. Dashed verticals mark the candidate start years used in the model scenarios (1977, 1988, 2001, 2016); the open circle marks the Thai DOF assessment start; the green diamond marks the DSIr optimum used for the DSIr group-window scenario (windows keeping at least 12 years). Horizontal bands give the interquartile range of DSIr from panel D for a stationary series with no depletion signal (grey) and for a series with a genuine level shift or trend (blue). Windows beginning inside the 1984–1990 transition score low on DSIr in the demersal and pelagic groups because the collapse years dominate the regression; windows beginning after about 2012 (grey area) retain a high DSI but collapse on DSIr because they are short. (D) DSIr for 200 simulated 30-year series of each type scored with the same engine: stationary, level shift, trend and hyperstable (CPUE proportional to B^0.4). The index separates a depletion signal from noise but does not separate genuine depletion from hyperstability, which is why the production model indexes abundance with the fishery-independent survey rather than commercial CPUE.
