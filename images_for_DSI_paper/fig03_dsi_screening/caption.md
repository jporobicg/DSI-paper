**Figure 3.** Data Suitability Index screening of candidate estimation windows. (A–C) DSI (light line) and DSIr (dark line) for every window that starts in the year shown and ends in the last observed year, for the anchovy, demersal and pelagic groups of the Thai Department of Fisheries (DOF) commercial statistics; each group is scored against its own effort series, and the shading between the lines is the robustness discount. Dashed verticals mark the candidate start years used in the model scenarios (1977, 1988, 2001, 2016; labelled above A). The open circle marks the Thai DOF assessment start; the green diamond marks the DSIr optimum among windows of at least 12 years, used for the DSIr group-window scenario. The grey horizontal band is the interquartile range of DSIr for stationary simulated series with no depletion signal (7–12; panel D). Starts in the shaded area on the right leave fewer than 12 years. Windows beginning inside the 1984–1990 transition score low on DSIr in the demersal and pelagic groups because the collapse years dominate the regression; windows beginning after about 2012 retain a high DSI but collapse on DSIr because they are short. (D) DSIr for 200 simulated 30-year series of each type, scored with the same engine: stationary (no signal), level shift, trend, and hyperstable (CPUE proportional to B^0.4). The interquartile range is 27–44 for the level-shift series, 29–46 for the trend series and 30–48 for the hyperstable series. The index separates a depletion signal from noise but does not separate genuine depletion from hyperstability, which is why the production model indexes abundance with the fishery-independent survey rather than commercial CPUE.
